Types of DOJ Grants

Discretionary Grants

Competitive awards. Solicitations state how many awards DOJ expects to make and the maximum amount.

Example: BJA Adult Treatment Court Program.

Formula Grants

Awarded by formula set in statute or appropriations acts. Recipients still apply and must meet specified requirements.

Examples: Byrne JAG State Formula; OJJDP Title II Formula Grants.

Congressional Earmarks

Congressionally directed spending for named recipients and specific projects, typically advanced through annual appropriations.

Use: One-year projects identified by Congress.

Cooperative Agreements

Usually discretionary awards for a public purpose with substantial federal involvement, often for training or technical assistance.

Examples: LETTAC; National Reentry Resource Center.

Note: Each office administers awards separately, with different requirements, accountability measures, and scope. Many DOJ grants provide three years of funding, and grantees can often reapply if the funding stream continues.
